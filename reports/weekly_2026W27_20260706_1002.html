BLINDSPOT OF THE WEEK

*Trump v. Slaughter* and the permanent restructuring of the American executive.

The birthright citizenship ruling was the wrong story to fixate on, and both sides fixated on it. *Trump v. Slaughter* — the reversal of *Humphrey's Executor* — is the ruling that will define executive-branch architecture for the next generation, and it received less than a quarter of the birthright citizenship coverage despite being structurally more consequential. Here is the concrete reason it matters more: the birthright citizenship ruling is reversible through legislation, ongoing litigation, or a future administration's executive order. The removal-power precedent, now established by a 6-3 court with no clear limiting principle on the Fed carve-out, is not reversible by any of those mechanisms. It requires a future court to affirmatively overturn it, which requires a future president to appoint enough justices to do so, which requires winning elections in an environment where the newly expanded removal power can be used to pressure independent agencies whose decisions affect electoral outcomes. The story is recursive in a way that the birthright citizenship story is not.

Left-side media covered it as one ruling among several in a busy SCOTUS term. Right-side media noted it approvingly but did not dwell on its structural implications — the administration had political reasons to perform disappointment about birthright citizenship and keep the structural win quiet. The result is that the most significant permanent change to American governance produced by the week's events generated less sustained analytical attention than a ruling that the administration had already identified workarounds for before the ink was dry. A well-informed analyst looking at this week's SCOTUS output in five years will consider the birthright citizenship ruling a footnote and *Trump v. Slaughter* the story.
